Demystifying LLMs 7

Part 1 · What it is

Attention: every token looks at the others

Same word, first meaning
Iateanappleforlunch

→ the fruit

Same word, second meaning
Applereleasedanewphone

→ the company

  • The token “apple” is the same in both; the context decides what it means
  • Attention: each token weighs all the others: “ate” and “lunch” pull toward the fruit, “released” and “phone” toward the company
  • That is how the model reads the whole context at once, to make its prediction
Lore

“Attention Is All You Need” (2017) introduced the Transformer, the “T” in GPT.

Part 1 · What it is

Inference: predicting with f(x) = y

f(token1 . token2) = distribution → token3
  • Once trained, the model is used for inference: given the text so far, it predicts the next token
  • x, the input: the tokens so far, called the context. It is all the model knows
  • y, the output: a probability distribution, a score for each token of the vocabulary (about 100,000)
f(“I drank a delicious cup of”) =
coffee42%
tea28%
matcha9%
cacao6%
99,996 more15%

True, and it makes people freak out. It tells you nothing about the quality of what comes out.

Instruction tuning: documents that teach a role

One of thousands of documents we wrote
User: What is the capital of France? Assistant: The capital of France is Paris. User: And of Italy? Assistant: Rome.

Reinforcement learning: a quiz with an answer key

Question · answer key: Canberra
What is the capital of Australia? attempt 1 Sydney ✗ attempt 2 Canberra ✓ attempt 3 Canberra ✓ attempt 4 Melbourne ✗
  • Questions with a known answer: the model tries several times, and each answer is checked automatically
  • Right answers are reinforced, wrong ones are not: it gets reliable on what it knows
  • It also learns to say “I don’t know” when unsure, so fewer hallucinations on well-known facts
  • Works wherever answers can be checked, maths and code too

Demystifying LLMs 26

Part 1 · Becoming an assistant

System 2 thinking: write the reasoning first

QuestionA man looks at a portrait: “Brothers and sisters I have none, but that man’s father is my father’s son.” Who is in the portrait?
Thinking… No brothers or sisters: an only child. So “my father’s son” can only be me. So “that man’s father” is me. Easy: the portrait shows me. Wait. then I would be my own father. No. If I am his father, he is my son. Answer: his son.
  • Models learn to write scratchpad reasoning before answering. The visible answer is the tip of a much longer hidden process
  • Why it works: each token is one pass of f, a fixed amount of computation. More tokens, more thinking
  • The text it writes becomes x: intermediate steps are kept, then rephrased, checked, corrected, or replaced by another strategy
  • Trained by RL on verifiable answers: thinking that leads to the right answer is reinforced
Lore

DeepSeek-R1 (Jan 2025): reasoning from RL in an open model. It shook the industry and the stock market.

Demystifying LLMs 28

Part 1 · The chatbot

The whole conversation goes back in, every time

f(system . user1) = assistant1 f(system . user1 . assistant1 . user2) = assistant2 f(system . user1 . assistant1 . user2 . assistant2 . user3) = assistant3
  • The model sees only x: it remembers nothing between two messages
  • So the chat system re-sends the full history on every message: the conversation is rebuilt from scratch each time
  • “Memory” features only add saved notes to x
KV cache

Reprocessing the whole history each time would be slow and costly. The model stores its computed attention states: only new tokens are processed, and cached tokens are billed at a fraction of the price.

Part 2 · Tools

Tool calling: the model writes, software runs

UserWill it rain in Paris on Saturday?
Assistant → systemweather(city="Paris", day="Sat")
System → assistant{"forecast": "rain", "temp_c": 11}
Assistant → userYes, rain, 11 °C. Plan indoors.
f(system . user . tool_call . tool_result) = assistant
  • A model that writes text can write structured tool requests
  • It runs nothing: it writes a request; the software executes and returns the result
  • A three-party exchange: user, agent, system. Two of them are usually invisible to you
