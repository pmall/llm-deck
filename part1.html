Part 1 · How an LLM writes

Think of an LLM as a lossy zip file of human knowledge

DeepSeek V3 (2025): ≈ 60 TB of web pages, books, code in → 671 billion parameters out.

Human text≈ 60 TB
The LLM file≈ 0.7 TB
Kept
What the text repeats: grammar, widely stated facts, common code patterns, writing styles
Blurred
What it says once: the exact wording of a page, precise figures, minor facts, sources

Unzipping is rebuilding: it regenerates a plausible text, not the original page.

Lore: Ted Chiang, The New Yorker (2023): “ChatGPT is a blurry JPEG of the web”.

Inference: one token at a time

  • One call reads the whole text so far and predicts the next token.
  • That token is added to the text, and the whole text goes back in for the next call.

How it becomes a chatbot

Ask a raw LLM “What is the capital of France?” and it writes “What is the capital of Italy?”. How does it learn to answer?

Part 1 · How it becomes a chatbot

Emergent property: the LLM picks up patterns

What is the capital of Italy? Rome. What is the capital of Spain? Madrid. What is the capital of Japan? Tokyo. What is the capital of Turkey? Ankara. What is the capital of Egypt? Cairo. What is the capital of France? Paris.
What is a dish from Italy? 🍕 Pizza. What is a dish from Spain? 🥘 Paella. What is a dish from Japan? 🍣 Sushi. What is a dish from Turkey? 🥙 Kebab. What is a dish from Egypt? 🧆 Falafel. What is a dish from France? 🥐 Croissant.
  • Unexpected: the LLM spots patterns in the prompt and continues them.
  • Nobody designed this ability. It emerged on its own as LLMs grew larger.
  • A prompt with a few examples steers the LLM. This is few-shot prompting.

Lore: “Language Models are Few-Shot Learners”, the GPT-3 paper (2020).

Part 1 · How it becomes a chatbot

Fine-tuning: one concept takes the lead

  • Pre-training absorbs everything people wrote, as much as possible.
  • Fine-tuning picks one coherent set of documents and continues the training on it.
  • The LLM keeps its knowledge, but that set now steers how it writes.

Part 1 · How it becomes a chatbot

Reinforcement learning: reward the right answers

<user> What is the capital of Australia?<end> <assistant> Sydney.<end> ✗ <assistant> Canberra.<end> ✓ <assistant> Canberra.<end> ✓ <assistant> Melbourne.<end> ✗ <assistant> Canberra.<end> ✓ <assistant> Sydney.<end> ✗
  • The LLM answers the same question many times.
  • A program checks each answer against a known one: Canberra.
  • Training then makes the right answers more likely and the wrong ones less likely.
  • Nobody writes the answers. The LLM finds them, and training keeps what works.
  • Only questions with a checkable answer qualify, like maths and code.
Demystifying LLMs · Part 1 16

Part 1 · How it becomes a chatbot

Human feedback: people’s picks replace the answer key

<user> Explain tokens to a child.<end> <assistant> Tokens are sub-word units of a BPE vocabulary.<end> ✗ <assistant> Tokens are little pieces of words, like Lego bricks.<end> ✓
  • No program can check clarity or tone. A person compares two answers and picks the better one.
  • Thousands of picks train a scoring model. It then grades answers the way the answer key did.
  • People prefer answers that agree with them, so the LLM learns to flatter.

Lore: April 2025: OpenAI rolled back a ChatGPT update that flattered users far too much.

Demystifying LLMs · Part 1 17

Part 1 · How it becomes a chatbot

Reasoning: the LLM writes its thinking before the answer

<user> A man looks at a portrait: “Brothers and sisters I have none, but that man’s father is my father’s son.” Who is in the portrait?<end> <assistant><think> No brothers or sisters, so I am an only child. “My father’s son” can only be me. So “that man’s father” is me. So the portrait shows me? Wait. Then I would be my own father. No. I am his father, so he is my son. </think> His son.<end>
  • Each token is one pass through the LLM, a fixed amount of computation. More tokens give more computation to the answer.
  • Each written step goes back in with the text. Later steps can check and correct it.
  • Reinforcement learning with an answer key rewards the thinking that reaches the right answer.
  • Chatbots often hide the thinking and show only the answer.

Lore: DeepSeek-R1 (Jan 2025) learned to reason this way in the open. It shook the industry and the stock market.

Demystifying LLMs · Part 1 18

Part 1 · How it becomes a chatbot

Chatting: one turn at a time

  • Each time you send a message, the chatbot sends the whole conversation to the LLM.
  • The LLM writes the assistant’s turn token by token, until the end token.
  • The LLM remembers nothing between turns. The conversation it receives is its only memory.
  • A hidden first turn, the system prompt, gives the assistant its instructions.
Demystifying LLMs · Part 1 19

What to watch out for

It now answers like an assistant, but still writes token by token from what it read in training. Where does that trip it up?

Part 1 · What to watch out for

LLMs are at their best in English

  • English dominates the web pages, books and code an LLM learns from.
  • More English text gives the LLM richer answers in English.
  • Fewer French texts give it thinner facts about French rules, procedures and jargon.
  • English‑speaking sources shape its norms, references and examples.
  • Asking in English, then translating, gives better answers in a less common language.

  • Chatbot usage limits are counted in tokens.
  • API prices are quoted per million tokens. Sent and written tokens have separate prices.
  • A written token costs about five times more than a sent one.
Demystifying LLMs · Part 1 22

Part 1 · What to watch out for

Spelling: an LLM does not see letters

  • Each token enters the LLM as a vector of thousands of numbers.
  • No letter is written in these numbers. There are no r’s to count.
  • Spelling backwards, rhymes and anagrams trip it up for the same reason.

Lore: In 2024, chatbots kept saying “strawberry” has 2 r’s. It became a meme.

Demystifying LLMs · Part 1 23

Part 1 · What to watch out for

The context window: a hard limit on the conversation

  • The window is the most tokens the LLM can read at once.
  • Text outside the context window does not exist for the LLM.
  • Windows grew from a few thousand tokens in 2022 to a million today.
  • Older LLMs lost details as the window filled up. This was called context rot.
  • Recent LLMs use the whole window far better.

Lore: Claude Opus 4.6 was asked to list every spell in the first four Harry Potter books (about 733K tokens). It found 49 of the 50.

Demystifying LLMs · Part 1 24

Part 1 · What to watch out for

Reflexive writing: no knowledge base behind an LLM

  • No table of facts sits inside the LLM. It writes every answer by reflex.
  • An invented answer is a hallucination. It is how an LLM works, not a bug.
  • Hallucinations hit precise, rare facts like case names, dates and citations.

Lore: In 2023, lawyers filed a brief citing this case and five others invented by ChatGPT (Mata v. Avianca).

Demystifying LLMs · Part 1 25

Part 1 · What to watch out for

Some answers cannot be predicted

  • Paris and the Varghese case are both predictions. One is right, one is invented.
  • Tomorrow’s forecast and your inbox were never in the training text.
  • No prediction can produce them. They must be fetched from a weather service or your mailbox.
Demystifying LLMs · Part 1 26

Part 1 · What to watch out for

Knowledge cutoff: an LLM stops at a date

  • Training stops at a date. Nothing written after it reaches the LLM.
  • Asked about a later event, it answers by reflex with older facts.
  • Newer facts must be added to the conversation, by you or by a web search.
Demystifying LLMs · Part 1 27

Part 1 · What to watch out for

A chatbot cannot leave the conversation

  • A chatbot reads the conversation and writes into it. Nothing else.
  • It cannot fetch the forecast or open your mailbox.
  • It cannot act either. No email sent, no file saved.
