Judge the document, not the mechanism

The worry

“It only predicts the next word.”

True, and it makes people freak out. It tells you nothing about the quality of what comes out.

The question that matters

Is the produced document good?

Useful, correct, fit for the task: that is what we evaluate, and what the rest of this talk builds on.

How the document gets written can change: other architectures exist, such as diffusion text models. Nothing that follows would change.

Why compression looks like intelligence

Newton
Three laws compress countless observations: falling apples, orbiting planets, colliding balls
Information theory
Predicting well and compressing well are the same problem
An LLM
To reproduce the world’s text, it must capture the regularities in it: grammar, facts, reasoning patterns

Intelligence is compression: the better an LLM compresses what humans wrote, the more it can do with it. That is how a text completer can become an assistant.

Part 1 · How it becomes a chatbot

In-context pattern matching

Dim: the prompt · gold: what the LLM writes
chat, cat maison, house arbre, tree fleur, flower
  • Show a few examples in the prompt: the LLM spots the pattern and applies it to the next case
  • No retraining, no instruction: the pattern is in the text, and continuing the text is all it does
  • It is emergent: nobody built this in, it appeared as LLMs scaled up
Lore

GPT-3 (2020), “Language Models are Few-Shot Learners”, the paper that made this the headline.

Demystifying LLMs · Part 1 14

Part 1 · How it becomes a chatbot

Instruction tuning: documents that teach a role

One of thousands of documents we wrote
User: What is the capital of France? Assistant: The capital of France is Paris. User: And of Italy? Assistant: Rome.
  • The fine-tuning data: conversations of a user and an ideal assistant, first written by paid people, now often generated by other LLMs
  • Fed to the LLM like any text, it learns the pattern (after User: comes Assistant:), which is pattern matching again
  • Now it answers instead of continuing, with a stable persona and tone
  • The base model never refused to help: it had no notion of being asked
Lore

InstructGPT → ChatGPT, 30 Nov 2022: the same model family, now speaking in turns.

Part 1 · How it becomes a chatbot

Reinforcement learning: a quiz with an answer key

Question · answer key: Canberra
What is the capital of Australia? attempt 1 Sydney ✗ attempt 2 Canberra ✓ attempt 3 Canberra ✓ attempt 4 Melbourne ✗
  • Questions with a known answer: the LLM tries several times, and each answer is checked automatically
  • Right answers are reinforced, wrong ones are not: it gets reliable on what it knows
  • It also learns to say “I don’t know” when unsure, so fewer hallucinations on well-known facts
  • Works wherever answers can be checked, maths and code too

Demystifying LLMs · Part 1 17

Part 1 · How it becomes a chatbot

System 2 thinking: write the reasoning first

QuestionA man looks at a portrait: “Brothers and sisters I have none, but that man’s father is my father’s son.” Who is in the portrait?
Thinking… No brothers or sisters: an only child. So “my father’s son” can only be me. So “that man’s father” is me. Easy: the portrait shows me. Wait. then I would be my own father. No. If I am his father, he is my son. Answer: his son.
  • LLMs learn to write scratchpad reasoning before answering. The visible answer is the tip of a much longer hidden process
  • Why it works: each token is one pass of the LLM, a fixed amount of computation. More tokens, more thinking
  • The text it writes becomes x: intermediate steps are kept, then rephrased, checked, corrected, or replaced by another strategy
  • Trained by RL on verifiable answers: thinking that leads to the right answer is reinforced
Lore

DeepSeek-R1 (Jan 2025): reasoning from RL in an open LLM. It shook the industry and the stock market.

Demystifying LLMs · Part 1 19

Part 1 · How it becomes a chatbot

The whole conversation goes back in, every time

llm(system . user1) = assistant1 llm(system . user1 . assistant1 . user2) = assistant2 llm(system . user1 . assistant1 . user2 . assistant2 . user3) = assistant3
  • The LLM sees only x: it remembers nothing between two messages
  • So the chat system re-sends the full history on every message: the conversation is rebuilt from scratch each time
  • “Memory” features only add saved notes to x
KV cache

Reprocessing the whole history each time would be slow and costly. The LLM stores its computed attention states: only new tokens are processed, and cached tokens are billed at a fraction of the price.

It now answers like an assistant, but still writes token by token from what it read in training. Where does that trip it up?

Part 1 · What to watch out for

Chatbots are at their best in English

80%

of pre-training data is English, roughly

The web is mostly English, and the data inherits it.

Quality gap

Richer prose, vocabulary and facts in English

Cultural default

Norms, references and examples skew English-speaking

Local knowledge

National rules, procedures and jargon are thinner

Ask in English, then translate: the smaller the language, the bigger the gain.

Part 1 · What to watch out for

The context window: a hard limit on the conversation

The conversation so far The answer being written Room left
  • The most tokens the LLM can process at once: the conversation and its answer
  • What does not fit does not exist for the LLM
Windows keep growing

A few thousand tokens in 2022, a million today

Context rot

The longer the conversation, the more LLMs used to lose track of what was in it, silently. Recent LLMs handle long contexts far better, but a big window is still not a perfect memory.

Lore

Claude Opus 4.6 was asked to list every spell in the first four Harry Potter books (about 733K tokens): it found 49 of the 50.

Demystifying LLMs · Part 1 25

Part 1 · What to watch out for

Knowledge cutoff: the LLM stops at a date

  • Training data stops at a fixed date: the LLM is frozen there
  • It does not know what happened since, not even today’s date
  • Ask the chatbot anyway, and it admits it, or guesses with the same fluency
  • Anything newer must be added to the conversation: by you, or by a tool

Part 1 · What to watch out for

Reflexive writing, not a knowledge base

Not a search engine: no lookup, no record retrieved. “Reflexive writing”, driven by the prompt.

Obvious continuation → looks like knowledge

The capital of France is …

Paris~99%
Open continuation → looks like creativity

I drank a delicious cup of …

coffee
tea
Hallucination

When there is no obvious continuation, it still writes one: plausible, confident, and factually wrong. Nothing in the chatbot checks that it is true.

Lore

2023: lawyers filed a brief citing court cases invented by ChatGPT (Mata v. Avianca).

Part 1 · What to watch out for

Some answers cannot be predicted

Behind the chatbot, the LLM is a frozen snapshot that predicts the next token. Some answers are not in it.

Not written yet

Saturday’s forecast, today’s prices

After the cutoff: not in the training data

Never seen

Your files, your inbox, your database

Private: never in the training data

Not guessable

An exact total, the r’s in “strawberry”

Needs computing, not predicting

The answer has to be fetched or computed, not predicted.
